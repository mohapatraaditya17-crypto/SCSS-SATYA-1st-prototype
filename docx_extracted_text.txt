1. Executive Summary
Sure Clue Scientific Solutions (SCSS) plans to enter the SEVA FIRST Innovation Challenge 2026 with SCSS SATYA, a forensic-led platform that helps authorised users screen digital images for possible manipulation and produces a clear, traceable examination record. SATYA is a working name; final branding is not yet locked.
Where we compete: Sector 9, Emerging Technologies (AI, Blockchain and Others), Sub-theme 4: Responsible AI, Deepfake Detection, Cyber Resilience and Digital Trust.
What makes it different: SATYA is not another black-box deepfake detector. It combines four things: image authenticity screening, an evidence integrity record, explainable findings, and review by a qualified human expert. The tool screens. The expert decides. Automated output is never presented as a final forensic opinion or legal proof.
Our strategy in one line: a forensic-led, explainable digital-evidence screening workflow that joins technical indicators with traceable documentation and human review.
Honest status: SATYA is a concept today, not a working product. Our entry is credible only if we can show a small, tested, working prototype by 16 October 2026. The plan below is built around that fact.

4. The Innovation: SCSS SATYA
SATYA gives investigators, insurers and forensic laboratories a fast, explainable first look at a digital image, and a record they can defend.
The problem. Manipulated and AI-generated images are now appearing in insurance claims, financial fraud, KYC checks and court evidence. Police, insurers and courts often lack a quick triage tool that explains its findings. Most deepfake detectors give a single score without showing how they reached it.
Our solution. SATYA uses passive-blind, non-invasive analysis. It does not need the original image, and it never alters the file under examination. This is the core idea from the founder's dissertation. The output is a structured report with indicators, test results, a hash log and clear limitations, and it can include a visual overlay of suspicious regions if the technical team can validate it.
How SATYA is different: four layers
Layer
What it does
Important limit
1. Image authenticity screening
Checks metadata, compression traces and image inconsistencies using methods the team can actually validate
We only include methods that pass our own tests
2. Evidence integrity record
Creates a SHA-256 hash, file ID, timestamp, tool and method version, and an activity log
A hash detects changes made after capture. It does not prove the image was original
3. Explainable findings
Lists which indicators were found, which tests ran, what the limits are and what an expert should review by hand
No black-box score
4. Human forensic review
A qualified reviewer assesses the findings and signs the final opinion
The tool alone is never a legal proof
What we will not build now
A universal detector for every type of deepfake.
Claims such as "99% accurate" without our own test evidence.
Blockchain, mobile forensics, video forensics and AI detection, all at once.
Any score whose scientific basis we cannot explain.
Adding these would increase complexity and risk a failed demo. A small tested product beats a large unverified one.
How we will present SCSS. Not as a forensic consultancy, but as an institution that combines domain expertise with a technology layer.
5. Prototype Scope (Minimum Viable Product)
The prototype is an image-first proof of concept. Audio, video and wider digital-evidence features stay on the future roadmap.
Priority
Features
Must have
Image upload and file validation; SHA-256 hash and metadata report; at least one technically defensible manipulation-screening method; a result page showing test results and limitations; a downloadable structured report with a reproducible test log
If technically feasible
Visual overlay or heatmap of suspicious regions; a suitable AI-generated-image detection model; a second independent method to cross-check results
Later roadmap
Video and audio integrity screening; institutional dashboard with role-based access; integration with government and insurance workflows
Methods the team may assess: metadata (EXIF) checks, JPEG double-compression, noise inconsistency, copy-move detection and an AI-generated-image classifier. We will only implement what the technical team can validate.
Validation rules
Use public benchmark datasets, such as CASIA, Columbia or CoMoFoD, only after we verify their licence and intended use.
Avoid dataset leakage between training and testing.
Report false positives, false negatives, the number of samples tested and the test conditions.
Claim no accuracy figure until we have measured it ourselves.
If a model does not work reliably, remove it from the demo.

10. Winning Strategy and Positioning
SCSS should not enter as one more generic AI detector. Our strongest position is: a forensic-led, explainable digital-evidence screening workflow that combines technical indicators with traceable documentation and human review.
Four advantages of this position
It connects directly to SCSS's existing domain expertise.
It keeps the prototype within a manageable scope.
It has clear relevance to government and institutional use.
It makes scientific limits and human accountability part of the product design.
What a first-place claim needs: working evidence, genuine difference from existing tools, and a feasible deployment plan. Without these three, the claim will not hold.
Presentation flow: problem in the Chhattisgarh and India context, then solution, demo, technology, impact and beneficiaries, SCSS's institutional background, scalability and finally our ask. Visuals will follow the SCSS navy-and-gold identity inside the official template.
